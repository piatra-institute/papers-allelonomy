# Research

Findings tiered per research-pipeline.md. Every citation verified against Crossref, OpenLibrary, or a bibliographic record before use; locators in sources.md. The seed chat (chats/chat.md) supplied the architecture, the concept's name, and its genealogy; its prose is not reused.

## The term's genealogy (T1)

- **Naniwada (1993)**, "A Japanese Perspective of the Transformation of Modern Civilizations," in the *Issues in Business Ethics* series (Kluwer), 153–171 (10.1007/978-94-015-8183-7_8): the documented prior use of "allelonomy," for mutual dependence against the image of reality as separate autonomous entities. The paper takes the word, credits the use, and adds what mutual dependence lacks: the four conditions.

## The emancipation and its mutation (T1)

- **Kant (1784)**, Berlinische Monatsschrift 4: 481–494: the exit from self-incurred tutelage; sapere aude.
- **Mill (1859)**, *On Liberty*: the self against society; the strongest liberal statement the mutation borrows from.
- **Arendt (1961)**, "What is Freedom?" in *Between Past and Future* (Viking): freedom among plural agents; if men wish to be free, it is precisely sovereignty they must renounce.
- **Balibar (2014)**, *Equaliberty* (Duke): freedom bound to the equal capacity to exercise and contest it.
- **Pettit (1999)**, *Republicanism* (OUP, 10.1093/0198296428.001.0001): non-domination; verified in a prior session.
- **Mackenzie and Stoljar (2000)**, *Relational Autonomy* (OUP): the normative base; verified in a prior session.
- **Castoriadis (1987)**, *The Imaginary Institution of Society* (MIT): autonomy as collective self-institution; institutions known as revisable creations.
